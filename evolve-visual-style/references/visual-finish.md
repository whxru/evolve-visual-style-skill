# Visual Intent and Finish Control

Use this reference to keep a candidate from becoming a polished version of a generic AI template. It governs design intent, asset commitment, within-artifact rhythm, contextual AI-tell detection, and screenshot-based finish repair. It does not define a house style.

## Contents

- Run calibration and design read
- Candidate thesis and ownability
- Asset strategy
- Section rhythm and concept spine
- Contextual AI-tell library
- Rendered finish audit
- Repair and regeneration rules

## Run Calibration and Design Read

Before assigning territories, infer four coarse dials from the brief. Use words or a small ordinal scale; do not expose fake numeric precision.

- `visual_variance` — restrained, expressive, or radical departure
- `visual_density` — sparse, balanced, dense, or deliberately variable
- `media_priority` — type-led, balanced, or image/material-led
- `task_clarity` — atmospheric, balanced, or conversion/task explicit

Write one private `DESIGN READ` that states the artifact kind, audience relationship, desired response, trust mechanism, and the main tension the search must resolve. Treat this as a run-level interpretation, not a visible eyebrow or explanatory paragraph in the demo.

Do not equate anti-default design with minimalism. Rich, layered, ornamental, commercial, playful, or highly technical directions are valid when their hierarchy and domain logic are deliberate.

## Candidate Thesis and Ownability

Before building, require each generator to commit privately to:

- `design_thesis` — one sentence naming what the visitor should notice or feel first and how the artifact will cause it
- `brand_idea` — one sentence stating what this visual argument says about this specific entity, subject, or product
- `concept_spine` — one governing semantic or material idea that can organize multiple choices without becoming repeated decoration
- `second_read_moment` — at most one subtle detail that rewards closer inspection without competing with the primary hierarchy

These are generative commitments, not judge-visible sales copy. A candidate is more ownable when its governing idea would lose meaning if transplanted unchanged to an unrelated organization. Do not reward novelty theater, obscure metaphors, or decorative lore that does not improve the requested artifact.

## Asset Strategy

Every search territory must declare one primary asset strategy:

- `photography` — documentary, product, portrait, architectural, archival, or other domain-relevant photography
- `generated-art` — an original image, material scene, spatial render, or other generated visual
- `illustration` — authored vector, ink, diagrammatic, painterly, or mixed illustration
- `collage` — visibly composed source material whose edges, overlaps, and scale shifts are consequential
- `product-real` — truthful product capture or interface evidence rather than a fake div-based screenshot
- `typographic` — intentionally image-light composition in which type, spacing, rules, and content carry the visual argument

Also state the asset's role—`evidence`, `atmosphere`, `narrative`, `explanation`, `navigation`, or `ornament`—plus its crop/framing and treatment. Across an open web batch, cover at least two materially different asset strategies. Do not force imagery into a type-led direction, but do not let every candidate fall back to CSS diagrams because they are easy to produce.

If a direction depends on imagery, weak, missing, fake, badly cropped, or incoherently treated imagery is a regeneration gate rather than a detail to excuse in metadata.

## Section Rhythm and Concept Spine

For a web demo, plan and then observe a compact rhythm map across its major zones:

- composition family and alignment
- density and empty-space budget
- image-to-text ratio
- focal scale
- background or material mode
- transition into the next zone

Avoid repeating one layout family by habit. Repetition is valid when it creates a purposeful index, catalog, table, feed, or other domain-authentic system. Otherwise vary tempo while preserving the concept spine. A static demo may still imply rhythm through two or three zones; it does not need to become a long marketing page.

## Contextual AI-Tell Library

Treat these as investigation prompts, not universal bans:

- generic centered hero followed by three equal feature cards
- repeated left-text/right-image zigzags or identical section shells
- oversized headline, prompt bar, and equal card row used as a transplantable bundle
- eyebrow labels on most sections, decorative numbering such as `00 / INDEX`, or status metadata with no product meaning
- decorative dots, version tags, city/time/weather strips, scroll cues, fake field notes, or fake photo credits
- fake terminals, dashboards, product screenshots, precision metrics, testimonials, clients, or research evidence
- cards used only to contain every idea, especially nested cards and uniform corner logic
- pills placed over images, gratuitous badges, hairline grids, crosshairs, orbital diagrams, or generic grain
- black or off-white ground plus one neon signal color used without brief or domain support
- every required role compressed above the fold to satisfy an imagined component checklist
- fashionable copy and startup names that could be transplanted into an unrelated product

For every flagged signal, record the visible evidence, why it is unearned in this brief, and whether a domain, reference, content, or functional reason justifies it. Penalize an unrequested bundle more than an isolated mechanism. Do not remove an appropriate centered hero, card grid, serif, pill, black background, or ornament merely because it appears in this list.

## Rendered Finish Audit

After the artifact is rendered and before cross-candidate convergence analysis, inspect the actual image or consistent screenshot set. Write one private `finish-audit.md` per candidate with `pass`, `repair`, or `regenerate` and concise evidence for relevant checks:

1. **Hierarchy and first read** — the intended focal order is visible; the thesis is not only written in metadata.
2. **Typography finish** — headline wrapping, orphans, line length, hierarchy, tracking, line height, weight, multilingual behavior, and text-image relationships look intentional.
3. **Spacing and optical alignment** — gutters, baselines, edge relationships, vertical rhythm, and negative space are controlled rather than merely token-consistent.
4. **Color and contrast** — role consistency, text legibility, CTA visibility, state distinction, and value distribution support the task.
5. **Geometry and surface** — radii, rules, borders, shadows, overlaps, texture, and light source form a coherent material model without requiring everything to match mechanically.
6. **Asset quality** — resolution, crop, focal placement, masking, image treatment, and relation to content are credible; no missing or fake substitute assets.
7. **Within-artifact rhythm** — major zones do not repeat one accidental composition family, density, or background cadence.
8. **Domain credibility and ownability** — content, hierarchy, evidence, and visual argument fit this kind of artifact and this particular subject rather than a generic design board.
9. **AI-tell evidence** — contextual signals are named with exceptions considered; do not apply a blacklist mechanically.
10. **Mechanical integrity** — no accidental clipping, overflow, broken navigation, unreadable required text, wrapping CTA, missing asset, corrupt render, or inconsistent comparison viewport.

For image candidates, translate web-specific checks into framing, focal hierarchy, edge quality, anatomy/object integrity, lighting consistency, text legibility, and output-resolution checks.

## Repair and Regeneration Rules

- Repair objective finish failures while preserving the candidate's thesis and unusual choices.
- Regenerate when the visible result does not express its thesis, depends on failed assets, violates hard constraints, or is fundamentally a generic template with renamed labels.
- Do not regenerate only because a design is polarizing, dense, sparse, ornamental, asymmetric, or unfamiliar.
- Do not let finish repair normalize every candidate toward the same spacing, radius, palette, card grammar, or minimalist taste.
- Complete finish audits before the population convergence audit. The first asks whether each candidate is visibly resolved; the second asks whether the candidates share an unrequested grammar.
